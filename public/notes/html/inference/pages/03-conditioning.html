Conditional Information & Prediction

Joint distributions, iterated expectations, and best prediction

ON THIS PAGE
  1. 1. Joint \(\to\) marginal \(\to\) conditional distributions
  2. 2. Conditional mean and conditional variance
  3. 3. Law of iterated expectations
  4. 4. Conditional expectation as the best predictor

Econometrics studies relationships between variables. The joint distribution is the starting point; conditioning turns information about one variable into a distribution for another.

1. Joint \(\to\) marginal \(\to\) conditional distributions

\[\begin{aligned}p_{X,Y}(x,y)&=P(X=x,Y=y),\\ p_Y(y)&=\sum_x p_{X,Y}(x,y),\\ p_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)&=\frac{p_{X,Y}(x,y)}{p_X(x)},\quad p_X(x)>0.\end{aligned}\]

Marginalization adds over the variable no longer being tracked. Conditioning restricts attention to a subgroup and renormalizes its probabilities to sum to one. For variables with a joint density, replace sums by integrals and use \(f_{Y|X}(y|x)=f_{X,Y}(x,y)/f_{X}(x)\) where the denominator is positive. Do not divide by \(\Pr(X=x)=0\) in the continuous case.

2. Conditional mean and conditional variance

\[\begin{aligned}m(x)&=\mathbb E[Y\mid X=x]\\&=\sum_y y\,p_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x),\\ \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X=x)&=\mathbb E[(Y-m(x))^2\mid X=x].\end{aligned}\]

\(\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=x]\) is a number for a fixed \(x. \mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]=m(X)\) is a random variable because the information \(X\) varies. Conditional variance measures dispersion around the relevant conditional mean, not the unconditional mean.

For the commuting table\(, \mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=0]=.5\) and \(\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=1]=.9\); the corresponding conditional variances are .25 and .09. Both the conditional mean and the conditional spread change with weather.

3. Law of iterated expectations

\[\mathbb E[Y]=\mathbb E[\mathbb E[Y\mid X]]=\sum_x m(x)p_X(x).\]

Assumption. \(\mathbb{E}|Y|<\infty\). Discrete proof. Substitute the conditional mean and multiply the conditional probability by \(p_{X}(x)\). The result is \(\sum _{x}\sum _{y}y p_{X,Y}(x,y)=\sum _{y}y p_{Y}(y)=\mathbb{E}[Y]\).

In the commuting example\(, \mathbb{E}[Y]=.5(.3)+.9(.7)=.78\). With multiple conditioning variables\(, \mathbb{E}[Y]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X,Z]]\). The nested form \(\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X,Z]\mid X]=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]\) averages away the additional information \(Z\) while retaining \(X\).

If h(X) is known conditional on \(X, \mathbb{E}[h(X)Y|X]=h(X)\mathbb{E}[Y|X]\), provided the relevant products are integrable. Therefore \(\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]=0\) implies \(\mathbb{E}[Y]=0\) and \(\mathbb{E}[h(X)Y]=0\). This implication is central to regression moment conditions.

中文直觉：先算每组平均数，再按各组人数比例加权，等于直接算总体平均数。不能不管组大小就简单平均。多重条件期望就是先分得更细，再逐步合并。

4. Conditional expectation as the best predictor

This chain is developed in SW Exercise 2.27 and made explicit here. Assume \(\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}]<\infty\). Let \(m(X)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]\) and \(u=Y-m(X)\). Then \(\mathbb{E}[u\mid X]=0\) and \(\mathbb{E}[u h(X)]=0\) for square-integrable h(X).

\[\begin{aligned}\mathbb E[(Y-g(X))^2]&=\mathbb E[u^2]+\mathbb E[(m(X)-g(X))^2]\\&\ge\mathbb E[u^2].\end{aligned}\]

Proof. Write \(Y-g(X)=u+m(X)-g(X)\), square, and take expectations. The cross term vanishes because \(\mathbb{E}[u\mid X]=0\). Thus \(m(X)\) minimizes mean squared prediction error over all square-integrable functions of \(X\); equality holds when \(g(X)=m(X)\) almost surely.

Setting the alternative predictor to the constant \(\mathbb{E}[Y]\) gives the law of total variance:

\[\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)]+\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[Y\mid X]).\]

For the commute table: \(.78(.22)=.3(.25)+.7(.09)+.3(.5-.78)^{2}+.7(.9-.78)^{2}=.138+.0336=.1716\).

中文直觉：知道天气后，组内仍有不可预测的波动；不同天气的平均通勤情况也有差别。总体波动=组内剩余波动+组间均值波动。回归就是把这种“用信息改善预测”的思路具体化，但最优预测不自动等于因果解释。

Question. If \(\mathbb{E}[u\mid X]=0\), must \(\operatorname{Var}(u|X)\) be constant?

Solution. No. Take \(u=X\varepsilon\) where \(\varepsilon\) is independent of \(X\) with mean zero and variance one. Then \(\mathbb{E}[u\mid X]=0\) but \(\operatorname{Var}(u|X)=X^{2}\).
